# Song of Songs 5:10 — Bright and ruddy

## Context

In 5:9, the daughters of Jerusalem ask what distinguishes the woman’s beloved from other beloveds. She begins her answer in 5:10 and continues with an idealized description of his appearance through 5:16. The following verses describe his dark hair, eyes, cheeks, lips, hands, body, legs, and mouth. Verse 10 introduces the portrait with paired appearance terms and a claim that he stands out among a myriad.



## Witnesses

The transcription below follows the Westminster Leningrad Codex (WLC), a pointed Masoretic text. This note does not attempt a full manuscript or versional collation.

## Transcriptions

**Masoretic Hebrew (WLC):**

> דּוֹדִי צַח וְאָדֹם דָּגוּל מֵרְבָבָה׃

**Consonantal text:**

> דודי צח ואדום דגול מרבבה

**Working transliteration:**

> *dōdî ṣaḥ wəʾādōm dāgûl mērəḇāḇāh*

## Translations

**Close working translation:**

> My beloved is bright and ruddy, distinguished among ten thousand.

“White and ruddy” is a traditional rendering. “Radiant/bright and ruddy” foregrounds the visual force of צַח. “Outstanding” or “preeminent” are conventional renderings of דָּגוּל; “awe-inspiring to behold” is a more interpretive proposal. The phrase מֵרְבָבָה is idiomatically “among ten thousand/a myriad,” not necessarily a literal numerical comparison.

## Variants and editorial reconstructions

The note follows the WLC wording; a full survey of ancient versions and manuscript evidence remains to be done.

## Lexical and grammatical notes

| Hebrew    | Transliteration | Form and syntax                                                                       | Working sense                                                                                                                                                                |
| --------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| דּוֹדִי   | *dōdî*          | Masculine singular noun דּוֹד with 1st-person common singular suffix                  | “My beloved.”                                                                                                                                                                |
| צַח       | *ṣaḥ*           | Masculine singular adjective                                                          | “Bright, dazzling, clear,” and in this visual context often “white.”                                                                                                         |
| וְאָדֹם   | *wəʾādōm*       | Conjunction וְ + masculine singular adjective אָדֹם                                   | “And red/ruddy.” It describes the beloved; it is an adjective, not the Lamentations verb אָדְמוּ.                                                                            |
| דָּגוּל   | *dāgûl*         | Masculine singular adjectival/participial form from דגל                               | Conventionally “distinguished, preeminent, conspicuous.” The root is associated elsewhere with banners; one proposal hears an awe-striking or arresting visual quality here. |
| מֵרְבָבָה | *mērəḇāḇāh*     | Comparative/source preposition מִן + feminine singular רְבָבָה, “myriad/ten thousand” | “Among ten thousand/a myriad”; translations also use “above ten thousand.”                                                                                                   |

The close colour pairing is **צַח וְאָדֹם** (*ṣaḥ wəʾādōm*), “bright/white and red/ruddy.” The second adjective belongs to the אדם colour family also represented by אַדְמוֹנִי (*ʾadmōnî*) for Esau and David, and by אָדְמוּ (*ʾāḏəmû*) in Lamentations 4:7. The forms are related but grammatically distinct: Song has an adjective, while Lamentations has a finite Qal verb.

## Codex Sinaiticus

**Greek (Codex Sinaiticus, 5:10):**

> λευκος και πυρρος

_leukos kai pyrros_

Literally: **white and ruddy** (or red).

The description continues:

- 5:11 — His head is gold; his locks are dark like a raven.    
- 5:12 — His eyes are like doves beside streams of water, washed in milk.
    
This is a striking combination:

|Feature|Description|
|---|---|
|Complexion|White and ruddy|
|Head|Gold|
|Hair|Dark as a raven|

## Reception and commentary

- JPS 1917 renders the line “My beloved is white and ruddy, pre-eminent above ten thousand.”
- The Revised JPS rendering “clear-skinned and ruddy” makes the complexion interpretation explicit.
- Chabad translates צַח וְאָדוֹם as “white and ruddy” and דָּגוּל מֵרְבָבָה as “surrounded by myriads.” Rashi first reads the verse plainly as a handsome young man who is white with a ruddy face, then gives an allegorical reading -  [Chabad, Song of Songs 5:10 with Rashi](https://www.chabad.org/library/bible_cdo/aid/16449/jewish/Chapter-5.htm)
- Bede quotes the Vulgate, **“Dilectus meus candidus et rubicundus, electus ex millibus”** (“My beloved is white and ruddy, chosen from among thousands”), then glosses it: **“candidus virginitate, rubicundus passione”** (“white through virginity, ruddy through the Passion”). He explains *candidus* as Christ’s sinlessness and *rubicundus* through the blood by which Christ washes away sins (citing Isa. 63:2 and Rev. 1:5). This is a Christological allegory in which the Song’s beloved is Christ; it is not a literal complexion gloss and does not identify Lamentations’ Zion or Nazirites with Christ. [Bede, *Expositio in Cantica Canticorum*, on 5:10](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Expositio_in_Cantica_canticorum).
- J. L. Andruska argues that דָּגוּל may communicate an awe-inspiring or arresting appearance, drawing on דגל in 6:4 and 6:10 as well as the banner/host imagery associated with the root. This is a proposal, while “distinguished/preeminent” remains a common translation.

## Interpretative questions

- Does צַח here primarily mean white/fair, bright/radiant, or a quality combining both?
- Does אָדֹם describe a red-toned/ruddy complexion, or a broader poetic impression of vitality and beauty? The Hebrew adjective itself does not specify blushing, cheeks, or a cause of redness.
- Is דָּגוּל simply “distinguished,” or does its military/banner association contribute an awe-striking visual nuance?
- The sequence “bright/white and ruddy” is a useful Hebrew parallel to Lamentations 4:7, where whiteness and redness also appear in a description of people. The parallel supports comparison of colour language; it does not by itself establish literary dependence or a fixed complexion convention.

## Claims supported

- Song of Songs 5:10 uses צַח and אָדֹם together to describe the male beloved in a positive, idealized portrait.
- Biblical Hebrew can apply red/ruddy language to human appearance without requiring that the whole person be uniformly red.
- The verse is a relevant comparison for the use of אָדְמוּ in Lamentations 4:7, while preserving the difference between adjective and verb.

## Uncertainties

- The precise shade or bodily feature intended by “ruddy” is not specified.
- צַח has a range that includes brightness, clearness, and whiteness; English “white” may narrow the image.
- The exact nuance of דָּגוּל in this verse remains debated.
- This poetic description is not direct evidence for a medical condition or a particular historical population’s appearance.
