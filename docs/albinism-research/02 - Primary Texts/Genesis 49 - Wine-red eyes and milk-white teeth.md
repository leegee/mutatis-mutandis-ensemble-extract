# Genesis 49:12 — Wine-red eyes and milk-white teeth

## Context

Genesis 49:8–12 is Jacob’s poetic blessing of Judah. Verse 12 uses wine and milk imagery in a compact description of eyes and teeth. It is a bodily red-and-white pairing, but it does not describe skin complexion; the images may also evoke abundance and prosperity in Judah’s blessing.

## Witnesses

This working note follows the pointed Masoretic text as represented by the Westminster Leningrad Codex (WLC). A full versional and manuscript collation has not been undertaken here.

## Transcriptions

**Masoretic Hebrew (WLC):**

> חַכְלִילִי עֵינַיִם מִיָּיִן וּלְבֶן־שִׁנַּיִם מֵחָלָב׃

**Working transliteration:** *ḥakhlîlî ʿênayim miyyāyin ûləḇen šinnayim mēḥālāḇ*

## Translations

**Close working translation:**

> Eyes dark/red from wine, and teeth white from milk.

English translations vary between “red-eyed from wine” and “eyes darker than wine”; the second half is consistently understood as milk-white teeth. The Hebrew uses *min* in both phrases, which can be represented as source (“from wine/milk”) or comparison (“than wine/milk”) in English.

## Variants and editorial reconstructions

No textual variant or editorial reconstruction is adopted in this note. The first adjective, חַכְלִילִי (*ḥakhlîlî*), is unusual and its precise nuance is debated; translations such as “red,” “dark,” or “flashing” should be treated as interpretive choices.

## Lexical and grammatical notes

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Form and syntax | Working sense |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| חַכְלִילִי | *ḥakhlîlî* | Rare adjective, modifying עֵינַיִם | Red/dark/flashing; precise shade uncertain. |
| עֵינַיִם | *ʿênayim* | Dual noun | Eyes. |
| מִיָּיִן | *miyyāyin* | מִן + יַיִן | From wine / than wine. |
| וּלְבֶן | *ûləḇen* | Conjunction + adjective | And white. |
| שִׁנַּיִם | *šinnayim* | Dual noun | Teeth. |
| מֵחָלָב | *mēḥālāḇ* | מִן + חָלָב | From milk / than milk. |

The pairing should not be conflated with the complexion language of Song 5:10 or Lamentations 4:7. Genesis concerns eyes and teeth specifically. Rashi reads the eyes as reddened by abundant wine and the teeth as whitened by abundant milk; other readings take the comparisons more directly (“darker than wine,” “whiter than milk”).

## Reception and commentary

- Chabad’s translation renders the phrase “red eyed from wine and white toothed from milk,” and its Rashi text connects the colours with drinking wine and milk.
- Modern translations differ over whether the eyes are red from wine or darker than wine, reflecting the rare first word and the force of the comparative/source construction.

## Interpretative questions

- Does חַכְלִילִי describe redness, darkness, sparkle, or an effect associated with wine?
- Are wine and milk the source of the colours, or standards of comparison?
- Is the verse chiefly a portrait of bodily beauty, or does its imagery also express agricultural abundance and prosperity?

## Claims supported

- Biblical Hebrew poetry places red/dark imagery and whiteness together in a single bodily description.
- Genesis 49:12 is an adjacent comparison for this project’s red-and-white imagery, but it is not direct evidence for a rosy complexion: its body parts are eyes and teeth.

## Uncertainties

- The uncommon חַכְלִילִי is lexically difficult.
- The precise relationship between the images and wine/milk is debated.
- This verse alone cannot establish a general Hebrew convention for complexion.
