# Leviticus 13 — Reddish-white skin lesions

## Context

Leviticus 13 gives priestly procedures for examining skin conditions and other suspected cases of *ṣāraʿat*. Verses 19, 24, and 42 combine white and red colour language in descriptions of visible marks on the skin or scalp. These are ritual-diagnostic descriptions, not ideals of complexion or claims about a people’s appearance.

## Witnesses

This working note follows the pointed Masoretic text as represented by the Westminster Leningrad Codex (WLC). It is a selective note on the colour phrases, not a full textual or medical study.

## Transcriptions

**Leviticus 13:19 (excerpt):**

> שְׂאֵת לְבָנָה אוֹ בַהֶרֶת לְבָנָה אֲדַמְדֶּמֶת

**Leviticus 13:24 (excerpt):**

> בַּהֶרֶת לְבָנָה אֲדַמְדֶּמֶת

**Leviticus 13:42 (excerpt):**

> נֶגַע לָבָן אֲדַמְדָּם

## Translations

The colour descriptions are commonly rendered “white, reddish” or “reddish-white.” In 13:19 the phrase describes a white swelling or a bright spot with a reddish cast; 13:24 applies the phrase to the appearance of a burn; 13:42 describes a white, reddish lesion on a bald scalp. Exact English phrasing varies among translations.

## Variants and editorial reconstructions

No textual variant or editorial reconstruction is adopted here. The note focuses on the Masoretic wording and colour morphology.

## Lexical and grammatical notes

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Form and syntax | Working sense |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| לְבָנָה / לְבָנָה | *ləḇānāh* | Feminine adjective agreeing with feminine noun | White. |
| אֲדַמְדֶּמֶת | *ʾăḏamdemet* | Reduplicated/frequentative adjective from אדם, feminine singular | Reddish, somewhat red. |
| אֲדַמְדָּם | *ʾăḏamdam* | Masculine form agreeing with נֶגַע | Reddish. |
| נֶגַע | *negaʿ* | Masculine noun | Mark, lesion, or affliction. |

The repeated אדם root gives a clear example of red language modifying or qualifying a white skin mark. The reduplicated forms are not the same morphology as אָדְמוּ in Lamentations 4:7 or וְאָדֹם in Song of Songs 5:10, but they belong to the same Hebrew colour family.

## Reception and commentary

English versions vary between “reddish-white,” “white and somewhat reddish,” and “white, somewhat red.” “Lesion” here is a neutral descriptive term; the biblical ritual category *ṣāraʿat* should not be equated uncritically with modern leprosy or a single modern diagnosis.

## Interpretative questions

- Does the Hebrew construction present a white mark tinged with red, or a conventional composite shade “reddish-white”?
- How does the repeated/reduplicated adjective nuance the basic אדם colour term?
- What can these ritual descriptions tell us about ancient colour vocabulary without treating them as complexion ideals?

## Claims supported

- Biblical Hebrew uses forms from אדם for reddish skin appearances and combines them with whiteness.
- Leviticus supplies lexical and descriptive parallels for red-white language on skin, while differing sharply in genre and evaluative context from Song 5:10 and Lamentations 4:7.

## Uncertainties

- English hyphenation and adjective order can obscure the Hebrew syntax.
- The phrases are descriptions within a ritual examination system; they should not be converted directly into modern clinical categories.
