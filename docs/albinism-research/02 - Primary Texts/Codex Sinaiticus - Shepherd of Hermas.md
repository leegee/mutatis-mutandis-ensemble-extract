## **1. The Shepherd of Hermas — Vision 4.2.1**

Direct white-hair reference

The Greek reads:

**εἶχεν δὲ τὰς τρίχας αὐτῆς λευκάς**

_eichen de tas trichas autēs leukas_

Literally:

"She had her hairs white."

The surrounding description is remarkable:

- She is a young woman (παρθένος).    
- She is dressed entirely in white.    
- She wears white sandals.    
- Her head is veiled.    
- Her hair is white.
    

Yet she represents **the Church**. Who?

This is not simply an elderly person with white hair. It is an apparently youthful female figure with white hair, carrying an allegorical meaning.

The passage is Vision 4, chapter 2, section 1.

## **2. The Shepherd — Vision 1 and Vision 3**

The white-haired woman is not a one-off appearance. She is part of a sequence of transformations.

Hermas sees the Church initially as an elderly woman:

- Vision 1.2.2: an elderly woman in shining garments, holding a book, seated on a great white chair.    
- Vision 2.4.1: the woman is identified explicitly as the Church, and her age is explained by the fact that she was created before all things.    
- Vision 3: she appears progressively younger.    
- Vision 4.2.1: she appears as a young woman, but retains her white hair.
    
This is particularly interesting because **white hair persists despite the transformation from old age to youth**.

`White hair + youthful female appearance + allegorical identity`


## **3. The beast's four colours — Vision 4.1.10**

The Greek is:

**μέλαν, εἶτα πυροειδὲς καὶ αἱματῶδες, εἶτα χρυσοῦν, εἶτα λευκόν**

- μέλαν — black    
- πυροειδές — fiery-looking    
- αἱματῶδες — blood-coloured    
- χρυσοῦν — golden    
- λευκόν — white
    
The four colour groups are interpreted in Vision 4.3:

| Colour           | Interpretation                         |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Black            | The present world                      |
| Fiery and bloody | Its destruction through blood and fire |
| Gold             | Those tested and refined               |
| White            | The coming age                         |

The final white is associated with purity and the future life of the elect.

## **4. Tobit 2:10 and 11:8–13 — white material in the eyes**

In the Sinaiticus recension of Tobit, the protagonist becomes blind after bird droppings fall into his eyes. The Greek describes the resulting condition using:

**λεύκωμα** (_leukōma_)

Meaning a white spot, film or opacity in the eye.

The sequence is:

- Tobit 2:10 — white films develop in his eyes.    
- Tobit 2:10 — physicians fail to restore his sight.    
- Tobit 11:8 — Raphael instructs Tobias to apply fish gall.    
- Tobit 11:12–13 — the whiteness is removed from the corners of Tobit's eyes and his sight returns.

The Greek text of Sinaiticus is particularly interesting because it preserves the longer recension of Tobit.
