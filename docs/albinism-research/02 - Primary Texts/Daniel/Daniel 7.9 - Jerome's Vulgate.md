# **Daniel 7:9 - The Ancient of Days and Hair Like Pure Wool**

## **Context**

Daniel 7 describes a vision of four beasts emerging from the sea, representing successive kingdoms. The vision then shifts to a heavenly court in which the Ancient of Days takes his seat.

Daniel 7:9 describes the figure:

- His clothing is white as snow.    
- The hair of his head is like pure wool.    
- His throne is fiery, with wheels of burning fire.

## **Transcriptions**

### **Masoretic Text (Aramaic)**

וְעַתִּיק יוֹמִין יְתִב לְבוּשֵׁהּ כִּתְלַג חִוָּר וּשְׂעַר רֵאשֵׁהּ כַּעֲמַר נְקֵא כָּרְסְיֵהּ שְׁבִיבִין דִּי־נוּר

_Transliteration (partial):_

_ve-ʿattiq yomin yetiv ... u-seʿar reʾsheh ka-ʿamar neqē ..._

### **Jerome's Vulgate**

> Aspiciebam donec throni positi sunt, et antiquus dierum sedit: vestimentum eius candidum quasi nix, et capilli capitis eius quasi lana munda: thronus eius flammae ignis ...

## **Translations**

### **English (literal)**

> I watched until thrones were set in place, and the Ancient of Days took his seat. His clothing was white as snow, and the hair of his head like pure wool. His throne was flames of fire ...

### **Jerome's Latin rendering**

 >  _vestimentum ... candidum quasi nix_

> _capilli capitis eius quasi lana munda_

|Term|Language|Meaning / significance|

|יוֹמִין (_yomin_)|Aramaic|Days|
|שְׂעַר (_seʿar_)|Aramaic|Hair|
|עֲמַר (_ʿamar_)|Aramaic|Wool|

|_antiquus dierum_|Latin|Ancient of Days|
|_capilli_|Latin|Hair|
|_lana munda_|Latin|Pure wool|

The comparison is noteworthy because the text does not explicitly describe the figure as an old man with grey hair. Instead, it uses a symbolic comparison with pure wool.

The imagery therefore permits several overlapping interpretations: age, eternity, purity, divine authority and judicial impartiality.

## **Reception and commentary**

### **Jerome -** **_Commentarii in Danielem_** **(c. 407 CE)**

Jerome's commentary provides an important early Christian interpretation of Daniel 7:9.

He identifies the Ancient of Days as the eternal divine judge.

His interpretation distinguishes the two principal elements of the figure's appearance:

|Feature|Jerome's interpretation|
|---|---|
|White clothing|Divine brightness|
|Hair like pure wool|Impartiality and uprightness of judgement|
|Ancient of Days|Eternal divine judge|

Jerome does not simply treat the white hair as a physical indication of old age. He gives it a moral and judicial significance.

**Daniel–Revelation connection**

Jerome also compares Daniel's vision with John's Apocalypse, particularly Revelation 4 and its vision of the heavenly throne.

This is significant for the subsequent interpretation of Revelation 1:14, where Christ's hair is described using imagery closely corresponding to Daniel 7:9.

Jerome therefore provides an identifiable early interpretative connection between the two passages.

## **Jerome's sources**

In the preface to his _Commentary on Daniel_ (c. 407), Jerome names several earlier Christian writers whose work he knew.

## **Interpretative questions**

1. Does the original Aramaic imagery primarily signify age, purity, judicial authority or some combination?
    
2. Does the description of white hair deliberately distinguish the Ancient of Days from an ordinary elderly human figure?
    
3. Why does Jerome interpret the white hair in terms of impartial judgement rather than simply antiquity?
    
4. Does Jerome's interpretation derive from an earlier Jewish or Christian exegetical tradition?
    
5. Does Jerome's explicit comparison with Revelation establish a direct interpretative connection between Daniel 7 and Revelation 1?
    
6. Does Bede preserve Jerome's judicial interpretation, or does he develop a different understanding of the whiteness?
    
7. At what point does the imagery become associated more strongly with divine eternity and immortality than with judicial impartiality?

## **Uncertainties**

- Exact wording and textual variants in the earliest Aramaic and Greek witnesses require further collation.
    
- The precise relationship between the Old Greek and Theodotionic versions needs investigation.
    
- The source of Jerome's interpretation of wool-white hair as judicial impartiality is not yet identified.
    
- Jerome's influence on subsequent commentators needs to be distinguished from parallel interpretation.
    
- Bede's direct engagement with Daniel 7:9 has not yet been demonstrated.
    
- The relationship between Bede's interpretation of Revelation 1:14 and Jerome's interpretation of Daniel 7:9 requires direct textual comparison.
