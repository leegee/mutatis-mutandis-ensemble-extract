# Daniel 7:9 — The Ancient of Days

**Textual language:** Biblical Aramaic
**Passage:** Daniel 7:9
**Status:** Core passage; lexical interpretation partly unresolved.

## Textual evidence

The Aramaic description includes:

- the Ancient of Days taking his seat;
- clothing described as white like snow;
- the hair of his head compared with clean/pure wool (depending on how the final adjective is understood).

Key Aramaic wording recorded in prior research:

`לְבוּשֵׁהּ כִּתְלַג חִוָּר ... וּשְׂעַר רֵאשֵׁהּ כַּעֲמַר נְקֵא`

Transliteration and exact pointing should be checked against the chosen critical edition before publication.

## Lexical issue

The adjective `נְקֵא` (*neqē*) has been discussed as potentially meaning “clean/pure”; the possibility of a wool-related sense such as “lamb's” has also been raised. This needs a proper lexical and textual review. Do not present one gloss as settled without citing the lexicon and context.

## Greek comparison

The Old Greek/LXX and Theodotion forms should be recorded separately rather than collapsed into a generic “Greek Daniel”. Prior notes record wording in the Old Greek involving white clothing like snow and hair like clean white wool; Theodotion has a related but not identical form. Exact text and edition references remain to be added.

## **Variants and editorial reconstructions**

The principal textual traditions preserve the central comparison:

- White clothing compared with snow.
- Hair compared with wool.    

The Greek and Latin traditions should be examined for differences in the precise adjective used for wool and the description of its purity.

Theodotion's version became particularly influential in Christian biblical transmission.

The relationship between the Masoretic text, the Old Greek and Theodotion requires further collation.
## Interpretative questions

- Does the passage use whiteness as a marker of age, purity, divine status, or visionary splendour?
- Is the Ancient of Days' hair necessarily a simple representation of ordinary old age?
- How do early Jewish and Christian interpreters read the figure?
- Is the wool comparison primarily chromatic, tactile, or both?

## Principle Christian Reading of White Hair

**Origen**

Divine radiance and heavenly brightness.

Earlier interpretation

**Jerome**

Impartiality and uprightness of judgement.

Explicitly attested, c. 407

**Bede**

Antiquity and immortality of divine majesty.

Explicitly attested, c. 703

## Evidence discipline

The text explicitly describes clothing and hair. It does not explicitly describe pale skin or unusual eyes. Do not extend the description beyond what is stated.
