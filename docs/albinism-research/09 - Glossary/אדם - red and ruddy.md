# אדם — red and ruddy

## Working question

Does אדם require an impossible, literal description of people as wholly red, or can it describe a pink/ruddy complexion? The Hebrew evidence supports “red/ruddy” as a colour term and makes a complexion reading natural in some contexts. “Blushing” or “flushed” is a plausible interpretation of an appearance, but it is more specific than the root itself.

## Forms and evidence in Biblical Hebrew

| Form                         | Passage                       | Context and significance                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| אָדְמוּ (*ʾāḏəmû*)           | Lamentations 4:7              | Qal perfect, 3rd plural of אדם, “they were/became red.” BDB glosses it here “ruddy.” This is the root’s sole finite Qal occurrence in Biblical Hebrew.                                         |
| אַדְמוֹנִי (*ʾadmōnî*)       | Genesis 25:25                 | Esau is “red/ruddy,” then described as hairy all over. A person can be called red/ruddy, though the verse does not say whether this means complexion, hair, or overall appearance.             |
| אַדְמוֹנִי (*ʾadmōnî*)       | 1 Samuel 16:12; compare 17:42 | David is “ruddy,” alongside יפה עינים וטוב ראי (“beautiful of eyes and good-looking”). The adjective describes visible appearance favourably; it does not specify cheeks or a momentary blush. |
| צַח וְאָדוֹם (*ṣaḥ wəʾādōm*) | Song of Songs 5:10            | The beloved is described as צח and אדום, then distinguished among ten thousand: a close poetic pairing of brightness/whiteness and redness for a person.                                       |
| מְאָדָּם (*məʾoddām*) | Nahum 2:4 [English 2:3] | Shields are reddened and warriors’ clothing is scarlet, anchoring the family’s basic red-colour sense. |
| יַאְדִּימוּ (*yaʾdîmû*) | Isaiah 1:18 | Hifil imperfect, 3rd plural from אדם; red/crimson sins in a symbolic red-to-white transformation, not a bodily description. |
| אֲדַמְדֶּמֶת / אֲדַמְדָּם (*ʾăḏamdemet / ʾăḏamdam*) | Leviticus 13:19, 24, 42 | Reduplicated forms meaning reddish; qualify white skin lesions in ritual-diagnostic descriptions. |

The forms are related but not grammatically identical: אָדְמוּ is a rare verb, while אַדְמוֹנִי and אָדֹם are adjective forms. The parallels illuminate the semantic range without settling Lamentations’ syntax.

## Comparative colour image in 1 Enoch

1 Enoch 106:2, 10 describes Noah’s body as “whiter than snow and redder than a rose” in the Greek tradition. The Qumran Aramaic fragment preserves a red-colour word in the description, but not the complete comparison. This is a useful parallel for the poetic compatibility of whiteness and rosy/red colour in a person’s appearance. It is not an occurrence of the Hebrew lemma אָדֹם and should not be entered as a direct cognate or Hebrew usage. See [[1 Enoch 106 - Noah's birth]] and [[Hebrew colour terms - semantic range and ruddy complexion]].
## What “ruddy” contributes

English “red” applied to a whole person can sound like a claim about uniform skin colour. “Ruddy” is a natural contextual gloss for a red-tinged human complexion or appearance. A blush or flush is one possible instance or cause of that appearance, but it is more specific than the Hebrew root אדם: no cheek, face, or act of blushing is named in Lamentations 4:7.

- **Lexical:** אדם denotes red colour; אָדְמוּ means “they were/became red.”
- **Contextual:** with human subjects, “ruddy” can describe a red-tinged complexion or attractive appearance.
- **Interpretive:** “blushing,” “flushed,” or “rosy” proposes a particular look or cause and needs support from the surrounding image.

## Application to Lamentations 4:7

The Masoretic sequence first compares the figures with snow and milk, then has אָדְמוּ followed by עֶצֶם מִפְּנִינִים, and then sappîr. The earlier white/bright imagery is part of the poetic context and may encourage a nuanced visual reading of “ruddy,” such as a pale appearance with a red or rosy cast. The verb alone, however, does not encode that contrast.

Keep the lexical and syntactic questions separate. אדם can mean “be red/ruddy”; it does not tell us whether עֶצֶם is literally “bone,” means “body/substance,” or functions adverbially (“in body”), nor exactly how מִפְּנִינִים qualifies the clause.
