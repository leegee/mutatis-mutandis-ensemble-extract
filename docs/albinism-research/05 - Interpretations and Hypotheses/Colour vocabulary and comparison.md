# Colour vocabulary and comparison

## Scope

This note gathers colour terms and comparative constructions across the initial dossier. It is not a lexicon and should not replace passage-specific analysis.

## Daniel 7:9

- Clothing: described as white like snow.
- Head-hair: compared with wool, with a disputed or nuanced adjective often rendered “clean” or “pure”.
- The text does not explicitly describe skin or eye colour.

## 1 Enoch 106:2

Reported Greek:
- `λευκότερον χιόνος` — whiter than snow.
- `πυρρότερον ῥόδου` — redder than a rose.
- `λευκότερον ἐρίων λευκῶν` — whiter than white wool.

Reported Aramaic:
- `שמוק` (*šmwq*) — interpreted as red/reddish.
- No Aramaic word for “white” is recorded as surviving in the relevant fragment.

Geʿez:
- `ጸዐዳ` (*ṣəʿəda*, verify spelling and vocalisation) is a point for lexical investigation.

## Revelation 1:14

- Head and hair are white.
- Comparisons: wool and snow.
- No explicit skin-colour description in the verse.

## Comparative grammar: *min*

The user's working question concerns the semantic and syntactic force of Aramaic/Hebrew *min* in a comparative or source-like construction. Preserve the alternatives rather than forcing an English gloss prematurely:

- “from / out of” (source or origin);
- “than” (comparative construction);
- “as / like” where supported by syntax and idiom;
- a possible intensifying comparative force in context.

A hyphen-like or linking function of a maqqef may be part of the user's proposed explanation, but the punctuation itself should not be treated as independently proving a comparative or intensifying meaning.

## Method

For each term, record:
1. exact script form;
2. vocalisation as printed;
3. transliteration convention;
4. lemma/root;
5. morphology and syntax;
6. contextual gloss;
7. lexicon and page/entry;
8. ancient translation equivalents;
9. confidence and competing analyses.
