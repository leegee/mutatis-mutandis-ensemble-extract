# Colour vocabulary and comparison

## Scope

This note gathers colour terms and comparative constructions across the initial dossier. It is not a lexicon and does not replace passage-specific analysis.

## Daniel 7:9

- Clothing: described as white like snow.
- Head-hair: compared with wool, with a disputed or nuanced adjective often rendered “clean” or “pure”.
- The text does not explicitly describe skin or eye colour.

## 1 Enoch 106:2

Reported Greek:
- `λευκότερον χιόνος` — whiter than snow.
- `πυρρότερον ῥόδου` — redder than a rose.
- `λευκότερον ἐρίων λευκῶν` — whiter than white wool.

Reported Aramaic:
- `שמוק` (*šmwq*) — interpreted as red/reddish.
- No Aramaic word for “white” is recorded as surviving in the relevant fragment.

Geʿez:
- `ጸዐዳ` (*ṣəʿəda*, verify spelling and vocalisation) is a point for lexical investigation.

## 1 Enoch as a comparative red-and-white body image

The Noah description is relevant to the Hebrew complexion passages because it pairs a human body’s whiteness with redness: Greek **λευκότερον χιόνος καὶ πυρρότερον ῥόδου**, “whiter than snow and redder than a rose” (1 En. 106:2; repeated at 106:10). This is a strong ancient parallel for a red/rosy quality coexisting poetically with whiteness in a person’s appearance.

Keep the evidentiary categories distinct: the Greek adjective πυρρός is not the Hebrew אָדֹם; the Aramaic fragment 4Q204 preserves only a red-colour word, not the complete snow-and-rose sentence; and the Ethiopic is a separate full witness to the Enochic passage. The parallel supports plausibility of the image, not a shared Hebrew lexeme, direct dependence, or an exact modern shade. See [[1 Enoch 106 - Noah's birth]] and Bautch, “Peter and the Patriarch,” pp. 24–26.

## Revelation 1:14

## Comparative grammar: *min*

The user's working question concerns the semantic and syntactic force of Aramaic/Hebrew *min* in a comparative or source-like construction. Preserve the alternatives rather than forcing an English gloss prematurely:

- “from / out of” (source or origin);
- “than” (comparative construction);
- “as / like” where supported by syntax and idiom;
- a possible intensifying comparative force in context.

A hyphen-like or linking function of a maqqef may be part of the user's proposed explanation, but the punctuation itself should not be treated as independently proving a comparative or intensifying meaning.

## Additional red-and-white comparisons

### Genesis 49:12 — bodily but not complexion imagery

Judah’s eyes are described with the rare חַכְלִילִי (*ḥakhlîlî*), interpreted as red/dark from wine or darker than wine; his teeth are white from/than milk. This is a genuine red/dark-and-white bodily pairing, but it names eyes and teeth rather than skin, so it is adjacent evidence rather than a direct complexion parallel. See [[Genesis 49 - Wine-red eyes and milk-white teeth]].

### Leviticus 13:19, 24, 42 — white and reddish lesions

Leviticus uses אדם-derived reddish adjectives with white marks on skin or scalp during priestly examination. These passages show red-white vocabulary applied to skin, but their ritual-diagnostic context differs from poetic portraiture and cannot be treated as an ideal complexion. See [[Leviticus 13 - Reddish-white skin lesions]] and [[אדם - red and ruddy]].

### Isaiah 1:18 — symbolic contrast

Scarlet/crimson sins become white as snow or wool. Isaiah is a useful contrast: it places red and white in a vivid transformation metaphor, not in a description of a person’s body. Its Hifil form יַאְדִּימוּ belongs to the אדם family but differs grammatically from Song 5:10 and Lamentations 4:7. See [[Isaiah 1 - Scarlet made white]].
