## **The underlying methodological fault line**

|Approach|Primary question|
|---|---|
|Textual criticism|What exactly did John write?|
|Philology|What do the Greek words and constructions mean?|
|Source criticism|Which earlier texts supply the imagery?|
|Jewish apocalyptic studies|What visual conventions were available to John?|
|Christology|What does the appearance reveal about Christ's identity?|
|Visual and cultural history|What would the description communicate as an extraordinary bodily appearance?|

The last approach is relatively underdeveloped in the commentaries here.

**A. The transformation of Daniel 7:9**

Does Revelation merely borrow the Ancient of Days' white hair, or does it deliberately transfer both the wool and snow imagery to the Son of Man?

**B. The relationship between divine and angelic appearance**

If angels and extraordinary humans can also have white hair, what makes this particular appearance distinctive, and how does that affect its Christological significance?

**C. The visual meaning of extraordinary whiteness**

**Is the description primarily about age, purity, luminosity, supernatural otherness, or something for which those categories are inadequate?**

Not to assume that these are mutually exclusive meanings. The challenge is to establish which are supported by the text and its historical comparanda, rather than importing a collection of familiar symbolic associations.

He provides a list that summarizes the parallels between Rev 1 and Dan 7 (Daniel, 172):
  Beale argues that the density of references to Dan 7 (and parallel material in Dan 10) indicates that Rev 1:8–20 is a “midrash” on Dan 7 and 10 and that allusions to other OT literature (Ezek 1–3; 8–11; 43) have been attracted to the infrastructure that Dan 7 and 10 have provided for Rev 1:8–20. In part this stems from Beale’s conviction that the author of Revelation does not allude to OT texts in an atomistic manner but is aware of the literary setting and context of those texts. This conclusion is unlikely, however, for the term “midrash” should be reserved for oral or written expositions of biblical texts, while the focus in Rev 1 (as in Rev 4–5; 13; 17) is not on interpreting Dan 7 but on the use of allusions to Dan 7 (and other OT passages) to legitimate the features of John’s vision. That is, Dan 7 and 10 appear to be not the object of interpretation in Rev 1:8–20 but rather a means of interpretation (cf. A. Yarbro Collins, JBL 105 \[1986] 734–35). A second weakness of Beale’s proposal lies in the fact that the sequence of motifs in the vision report of Rev 1:9–20 is largely traditional and that even Dan 7 and 10 (perhaps also Ezek 1–3) were very probably structured in conformity with an existing visionary schema. Another problem lies in the fact that Beale does not address those scholars who have argued that Ezekiel provided the major structural model for Revelation (e.g., Boismard, RB 56 \[1949] 507–41; Vanhoye, Bib 43 \[1962] 436–76); both hypotheses cannot be true.

Koester associates the imagery with both Daniel 7:9 and Daniel 10:5–6.

|Feature in Revelation 1|Koester's proposed relationship|
|---|---|
|White hair|Ancient of Days, Daniel 7:9|
|Fiery eyes|Divine or angelic power; Daniel 10:6 and 1 Enoch 106|
|Feet like shining bronze|Daniel 10:6, particularly the Greek Theodotion|
|Voice like rushing water|Theophanic imagery in Ezekiel 1:24 and 43:2|

His discussion of _chalkolibanos_ in verse 15 proposes that John has coined a word by modifying the Greek expression for shining bronze in Daniel 10:6. This is a more precise account of literary transformation than simply saying Revelation borrows Danielic imagery.

He establishes that white hair occurs in descriptions of "God" and angels, but doesn't really investigate the visual phenomenon across those descriptions. Are we dealing with the same kind of whiteness? Does it signify age, luminosity, purity, supernatural otherness, or some combination? Does the comparison with wool contribute something different from the comparison with snow?

And there is a further distinction worth preserving:

- Daniel 7:9 describes the Ancient of Days with hair like pure wool.
- Revelation 1:14 describes the head and hair as white like wool and snow.
- 1 Enoch 106 describes Noah's extraordinary appearance through a combination of white hair, bodily whiteness and red colouring.
