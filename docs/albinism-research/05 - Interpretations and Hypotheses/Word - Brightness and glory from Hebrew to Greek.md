## Weight - כָּבוֹד -  *kāḇōḏ* / _kavod_

Hebrew noun · masculine · Strong's H3519

**Root:** כ־ב־ד (K-B-D)

**Basic semantic idea:** weight, heaviness, substance.
Extended meanings: honour, dignity, reputation, wealth, splendour, glory.

The root is also found in the adjective _kāvēḏ_ (כָּבֵד), meaning heavy, and the verb _kāḇaḏ_ (כָּבַד), meaning to be heavy or weighty, and in certain grammatical forms to honour or treat as important.

For example is Exodus 20:12:

**כַּבֵּד אֶת־אָבִיךָ וְאֶת־אִמֶּךָ**

_Kabbed et-avikha ve-et immekha_ — "Honour your father and your mother."

The connection is conceptually quite natural: to honour someone is to give them weight, importance and standing. The opposite idea is lightness in the sense of insignificance or dishonour.

> The glory of God is a key theological term in the Old Testament that has several interrelated core meanings: (1) dignity, high position, honour visibly expressed in wealth or external splendour; (2) respect or reverence; (3) object of respect; (4) God’s manifest presence; (5) means of referring to oneself (‘my glory’; Collins 1997).
                                                           -- Schneider

## From "weight" to visible "glory"

**Genesis 31:1 - material wealth**

Jacob's possessions are described as his _kavod_. In this context, "wealth" or "riches" is the appropriate meaning. It is the material substance of his prosperity.

**Exodus 16:10 - divine manifestation**

The Israelites look towards the wilderness, and the glory of the Lord appears in the cloud.

**Exodus 24:16–17 - Sinai**

The glory of the Lord settles upon Mount Sinai. The appearance of that glory is like a consuming fire at the summit.

- A person's _kavod_: their wealth, status or standing.    
- A king's _kavod_: his majesty and splendour.    
- God's _kavod_: his overwhelming majesty and manifest presence.    
- God's manifest presence: something that can be perceived through fire, cloud, light and other extraordinary phenomena.

## Hebrew has a word for "light"

**אוֹר —** **_ʾor_**

It is the word used in Genesis 1:3:

יְהִי אוֹר — _Yehi ʾor_ — "Let there be light."

This gives us a useful distinction:

|Hebrew|Basic meaning|Relevance|
|---|---|---|
|כָּבוֹד (_kavod_)|Glory, honour, weight, splendour|Divine majesty and presence|
|אוֹר (_or_)|Light|Physical or metaphorical illumination|
|אוֹרָה (_orah_)|Light, illumination|Related light vocabulary|
|נוֹגַהּ (_nogah_)|Brightness, radiance, shining|Particularly interesting for descriptions of luminous appearance|
|זֹהַר (_zohar_)|Radiance, splendour|Later important in Jewish mystical writing|

These are not synonyms. But they can occur together.

Isaiah 60:1 is a particularly good example:

קוּמִי אוֹרִי כִּי בָא אוֹרֵךְ וּכְבוֹד יְהוָה עָלַיִךְ זָרָח

_Qumi ori ki va ʾorekh, u-khvod YHWH ʿalayikh zarakh._

"Arise, shine, for your light has come, and the glory of the LORD has risen upon you."

Here _or_ and _kavod_ occur in the same sentence. The light comes; the glory of the Lord rises upon Jerusalem.

Thus the Hebrew tradition can associate divine glory with visible illumination without making _kavod_ itself a synonym for light.

## Visible Glory - Ezekiel 1:28

Ezekiel's extraordinary vision concludes:

"כֵּן מַרְאֵה דְּמוּת כְּבוֹד־יְהוָה"
_Ken marʾeh demut-kəvod YHWH_
Such was the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the LORD.

The text explicitly uses the language of **appearance** (_marʾeh_) and **likeness** (_demut_) to describe the _kavod_.

## **Septuagint: how** **_kavod_** **becomes** **_doxa_**

The translators frequently rendered _kavod_ as:

**δόξα** - _doxa_

In classical Greek, _doxa_ generally means opinion, reputation, estimation or honour. In the Septuagint, it became one of the principal words for the glory of God.

**Exodus 16:10 Hebrew**
כְּבוֹד יְהוָה נִרְאָה בֶּעָנָן
_Kəvod YHWH nirʾah beʿanan_
The glory of the LORD appeared in the cloud.

**Exodus 16:10 Septuagint**
ἡ δόξα Κυρίου ὤφθη ἐν τῇ νεφέλῃ
_Hē doxa Kyriou ōphthē en tē nephelē_
The glory of the Lord appeared in the cloud.

**Luke 2:9 Greek**
δόξα Κυρίου περιέλαμψεν αὐτούς
_Doxa Kyriou perielampsen autous_
The glory of the Lord shone around them.
