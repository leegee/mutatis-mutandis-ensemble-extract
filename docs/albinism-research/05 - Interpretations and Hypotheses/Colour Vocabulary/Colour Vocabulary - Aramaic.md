# *šmwq* / _šemêq_

In [[1 Enoch 106 - Noah's birth]].

The term **šmwq** (Aramaic _šemêq_) appears in **1 Enoch 18:14** and **21:5** within the **Aramaic fragments** of the **Book of Watchers** (found in **Qumran Cave 4**: **4Q201** and **4Q202**).

In these passages, **šmwq** is translated as **"furnace"** or **"smelting furnace"** (Hebrew _kîr_), describing the place where the fallen angels are bound and tortured by the archangels. The rendering is straightforward since **šmwq** (Aramaic, cf. Hebrew _kîr_) is the standard Semitic word for a smelting furnace, and all major editions agree on this translation.

The furnace functions as a **two-stage eschatological image**: in the Book of the Watchers it destroys the Nephilim spirits (a pre-Flood event), while in the Parables it becomes the final destination of the Watchers at the end of days. The Aramaic _šmwq_ in the Qumran fragments corresponds to the 10:13 usage.

See **Józef Milik**, in his 1976 edition _The Books of Enoch: Aramaic Fragments of Qumrân Cave 4_ (Oxford: Clarendon Press)

The most recent comprehensive edition, **Henryk Drawnel** (in consultation with **Émile Puech**), _Qumran Cave 4: The Aramaic Books of Enoch_ (Oxford: OUP, 2019), also renders it as "furnace."

**Michael Langlois** (_The First Manuscript of the Book of Enoch_, Paris: Cerf, 2008) likewise uses "furnace" in his study of 4Q201.
