> An alternative is to understand גִּזְרָתָם (_gizratām_) in relation to the root גזר (_g-z-r_), ‘to cut’ or ‘divide’, and to take the comparison with sapphire as referring to the subjects' shaping or form. The KJV's ‘polishing’ reflects an interpretation of this kind, associating the word with the cutting or finish of a precious stone. The reading has some contextual appeal: it is close to the root's basic sense, requires no emendation, and fits the chapter's earlier imagery of precious materials in 4:1–2. Other translations, including the RSV's ‘form’ and Luther's ‘appearance’, offer related understandings, while the Septuagint's ‘pieces’ may also be compatible with a sense of shape or configuration. The lexical evidence is nevertheless uncertain. The noun is rare, and its other occurrences, principally in Ezekiel 41–42, refer to a separated area rather than to the form of a person. BDB's proposal of ‘cutting’ or ‘polishing’ is itself conjectural, inferred partly from the reference to a precious stone. The interpretation therefore deserves consideration alongside the proposed references to hair and veins, but cannot be established from the root alone.

I would then add your point about _tōʾar_ separately, perhaps at the end of the Blue section or in the contextual discussion:

> This reading also offers a possible connection between the end of verse 7 and the beginning of verse 8. If _gizratām_ refers to the subjects' form or shape, its juxtaposition with תֹּאַר (_tōʾar_), ‘form’ or ‘appearance’, at the beginning of verse 8 creates a degree of continuity between the descriptions. The two terms need not refer to the same physical feature.
