# Research Questions

## Core question

How are exceptional combinations of hair-colour, skin-colour, and unusual eyes represented in texts, and how do those descriptions change through copying, translation, commentary, and later literary reuse?

## Initial biblical and para-biblical questions

1. What exactly is described in Daniel 7:9: garment, head-hair, or both?
2. Does Daniel 7's wool comparison encode colour alone, purity, texture, age, or more than one quality?
3. What does Daniel 10:5–6 contribute, and what should not be inferred from its luminous/metallic imagery?
4. In 1 Enoch 106, what is actually preserved in Aramaic, what is preserved in Greek, and what appears only in editorial reconstruction?
5. Does the Greek Noah description represent a faithful rendering of a Semitic source, a translation choice, or an expanded interpretation?
6. What can be established about the Geʿez wording and the history of its translation?
7. What does the earliest physical witness to Revelation 1:14 actually preserve?
8. How do later translations and commentaries add, remove, or reinterpret narrative and visual details?

## Methodological questions

- How can composition, redaction, translation, copying, and reception be dated independently?
- Which colour terms are literal, comparative, symbolic, or conventional?
- When does a text describe a person rather than an object, garment, light source, or metaphor?
- What is the difference between a manuscript reading and an editor's reconstruction?
- What evidence would justify a claim of influence between Daniel, Enoch, Revelation, and later texts?
