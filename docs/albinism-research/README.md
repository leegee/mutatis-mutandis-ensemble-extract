# Extraordinary Paleness Obsidian Research Vault

A source-centred research archive for the investigation of extraordinary paleness of hair, skin and eyes in English writing, with an initial textual dossier on Daniel, 1 Enoch (Noah), and Revelation.

## Research principles

This archive deliberately separates:

1. **Composition** — when a work or textual unit may have been composed.
2. **Redaction** — when a textual form may have taken shape.
3. **Translation** — when a text was rendered into another language.
4. **Physical witness** — the date and nature of the surviving manuscript or papyrus.
5. **Editorial reconstruction** — text supplied by modern editors where the witness is damaged or lacunose.
6. **Reception and interpretation** — how readers and commentators understood the passage.
7. **Research hypothesis** — the investigator's proposed explanation, which must not be silently promoted to fact.

A date attached to a manuscript is not automatically the date of composition or translation. Where exact dates or readings are uncertain, the notes say so.

## Status vocabulary

- `established`: supported by the surviving witness or strong consensus.
- `probable`: plausible and supported, but not certain.
- `tentative`: a working proposal requiring further checking.
- `unverified`: included as a research lead, not yet independently checked against an edition or catalogue.
- `disputed`: competing readings or interpretations exist.
- `editorial`: supplied or normalised by an editor rather than securely visible in the artefact.

## Workflow

- Give each physical manuscript or papyrus its own note in `03 - Manuscript Witnesses`.
- Give each textual passage its own note in `02 - Primary Texts`.
- Record each reading with a link to its witness and edition.
- Put interpretative arguments in `05 - Interpretations and Hypotheses`, not in the witness transcription.
- Add bibliography entries in `07 - Bibliography` and link them from the relevant notes.
- Update the chronology only when the type of date is clear.
