

## Wycliffe

**Wycliffe Early Version (c. 1382):**

> **Sai.** Whittere is Nazareis þan ſnoȝ, ſhynendere þan mylc; reddere þan old yuer, þan ſafyr fairere.

**Wycliffe Later Version (c. 1395, Purvey):**

> **Zai.** Nazareis therof weren whitere than snow, schynyngere than mylk; rodier than elde yuer, fairere than safire.

## **Coverdale (1535)** — the source text for Lamentations in the Matthew and Great Bibles:

> 7 Her Nazarites were purer then the snowe, & whiter then ye milke: they were more ruddie in bodie, then the redde precious stones: they were like polished saphir.  

**Matthew Bible (1537)** and **Great Bible (1539)**: The text is identical to Coverdale's, since Tyndale had not translated this book and both simply carried forward his work.

## **Geneva Bible (1560):**

> 7 Her Nazarites were purer then the snowe, and whiter then ye milke: they were more ruddie in bodie, then the redde precious stones; they were like polished saphir.  

(The only difference from Coverdale is "they can not knowe them" vs. "they are not knowen" in v. 8.)


## **Bishops' Bible (1568):**

> 7 Her abstayners were whyter then the snowe or milke, their colour was freshe, red as corall, their beautie like the Saphire.

The Bishops' Bible departs noticeably here — "faces be very blacke" instead of "visage is blacker than a coal," and the more elaborate "insomuche that thou shouldest not knowe them." Verse 7 follows the same basic pattern as the Geneva.


## **KJV (1611)** — for comparison, since the KJV translators were instructed to revise the Bishops' Bible:

> 7 Her Nazarites were purer than snow, they were whiter than milk, they were more ruddy in body than rubies, their polishing was of sapphire:  

The KJV essentially **reverted** to the Geneva/Coverdale wording for these verses, discarding the Bishops' "faces be very blacke" in favor of the older "visage is blacker than a coal."
