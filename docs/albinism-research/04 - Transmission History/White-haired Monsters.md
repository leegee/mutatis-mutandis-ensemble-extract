

## The Tiddy Man (English Fens)

> Tiddy Mun was a legendary bog spirit associated with England's fenland, believed to control the waters and mists of the Fens of South Lincolnshire, the Carrs of North Lincolnshire and the fens of the Isle of Ely. Folklorist **M. C. Balfour** recorded the belief in **1891**, describing him as no bigger than a three-year-old child but with the appearance of an **old man, with long tangled white hair** and a matted beard, dressed in a grey gown that made him hard to see at dusk. Stories vary in tone: he could bring pestilence when angered by drainage, yet villagers also called on him during floods to make the waters recede. The name denotes an individual spirit rather than a category of beings.

## Yeti (Himalayan)

> The Yeti is a mythical, large, hairy, ape-like or humanoid creature said to inhabit the Himalayan mountains of Asia, particularly Nepal, Tibet, Bhutan and neighbouring regions, and it is sometimes called the Abominable Snowman in Western popular culture. Accounts describe it as bipedal and covered in brown, grey or **white hair**, and in Tibetan lore it appears in several varieties of **differing size and fur colour**. Within Himalayan traditions it is sometimes linked to the wild man of the snows, and in Tibetan Buddhism it is treated as a nonhuman being that may follow the Dharma. Sightings, footprints and alleged scalps have been reported, though many are considered hoaxes or misidentified animals such as bears.
