### **A. Head and hair: antiquity, immortality and the elect**

> _Caput autem ejus et capilli erant candidi, etc._
> 
> _Antiquitas et immortalitas majestatis in capite candor ostenditur, cui praecipui quique velut capilli adhaerentes, propter oves ad dexteram futuras, instar lanae, et propter dealbatorum innumerabilem turbam, et electorum a coelo datorum, instar nivis effulgent._

Machine translation:

> The antiquity and immortality of majesty are shown by the whiteness of the head. The outstanding individuals, adhering to it like hairs, shine like wool because of the sheep who will be placed at the right hand, and like snow because of the innumerable multitude of those made white, the elect given from heaven.

Reading notes:

- The wool comparison is associated with the sheep at Christ's right hand (Matthew 25).
- The snow comparison is associated with the innumerable multitude of the elect, described as _dealbati_ — **made** white or whitened.
- _candidus_ describes the brightness or whiteness, while _dealbatus_ describes the elect as having been made white.

### **B. Eyes: preachers, spiritual illumination and judgement**

Bede writes:

> _Et oculi ejus velut flamma ignis._
> 
> _Oculi Domini praedicatores sunt, igne spirituali, et fidelibus lumen, incredulis praebentes incendium._

Machine translation:

> And his eyes were like a flame of fire. The eyes of the Lord are the preachers, who, with spiritual fire, provide light for the faithful and a consuming fire for unbelievers.

A [modern comparison in Latin](https://www.documentacatholicaomnia.eu/03d/0627-0735%2C_Beda_Venerabilis%2C_Explanatio_Apocalypsis%2C_LT.pdf) of the commentaries identifies this particular explanation as material Bede took over from Primasius.

### **C. Feet: the Church tested by persecution**

Bede writes:

> _Et pedes ejus similes orichalco sicut in camino ardenti._
> 
> _Pedes ignitos, novissimi temporis Ecclesiam dicit, quae vehementibus est examinanda et probanda pressuris. Orichalcum quippe est aes multo igne et medicamine perductum ad aureum colorem._

Machine ranslation:

> And his feet were like orichalcum, as in a burning furnace. By the fiery feet he means the Church of the last time, which must be examined and tested by severe tribulations. For orichalcum is bronze brought to a golden colour by much fire and by treatment with certain substances.

ChatGPT:

This gives us several distinct ideas:

1. The feet represent the Church of the last age.
    
2. Their fiery appearance represents the persecution and tribulation through which that Church must pass.
    
3. _Orichalcum_ is explained as a copper alloy transformed by fire and treatment until it acquires a golden colour.
    

Bede is therefore not simply describing a metal that happens to be bright. He gives an account of its manufacture and uses that transformation as an image of the Church being tested.

He also mentions an alternative reading, _orichalco Libani_ ("orichalcum of Lebanon"), which he interprets geographically and ecclesiologically, associating Lebanon with the persecution of the Church in Judaea. This is a valuable textual and interpretative detail to retain separately from his main reading
