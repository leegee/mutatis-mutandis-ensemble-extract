# Codex Sinaiticus – א – GA 01

**Papyrus 18 (𝔓18) is often listed as the next early papyrus of Revelation**, and is dated to the third century, but it preserves Revelation 1:4–7. It does not contain the descriptions of the hair, eyes or feet in 1:14–15. So it is not the next witness to the passage we are examining

[Text](https://codexsinaiticus.org/en/manuscript.aspx?book=59&chapter=1&lid=en&side=r&zoomSlider=0)

## Identification

- **Work:** New Testament, Revelation (Ἀποκάλυψις Ἰωάννου), including Revelation 1:14–15.
    
- **Language:** Ancient Greek (Koine Greek).
    
- **Repository:** Principally the British Library, London; other portions are held by Leipzig University Library, the National Library of Russia, and Saint Catherine's Monastery, Sinai.
    
- **Shelf mark / inventory:** British Library Add MS 43725 (principal surviving portion). The manuscript is Gregory–Aland 01, conventionally designated א.
    
- **Material / format:** Parchment codex, written in Greek uncial script.
    
- **Date range:** Conventionally mid-fourth century CE. A more cautious modern assessment allows a range from early fourth to early fifth century.
    
- **Dating basis:** Palaeographical assessment of the handwriting. The conventional date is not based on a dated colophon.
    
- **Textual coverage:** Revelation 1:14–15 survives in the manuscript. Codex Sinaiticus contains the whole book of Revelation, as well as the New Testament, substantial portions of the Old Testament, the Epistle of Barnabas and part of the Shepherd of Hermas.
    

## Physical description

A large, carefully produced parchment codex. The British Library's principal surviving portion comprises 347 leaves, measuring approximately 380 × 345 mm.

The codex was originally much larger. It probably contained at least 730 leaves, although its precise original extent is reconstructed.

The prose books are laid out in four columns per page, with approximately 48 lines per column. The arrangement produces an unusually spacious and visually distinctive page.

The parchment is notably fine and relatively uniform. The manuscript's production involved substantial preparation and selection of writing material.

The codex was copied by at least three scribes and subsequently corrected by a number of hands.

## Textual evidence

### Diplomatic transcription

The following reproduces the electronic transcription of Codex Sinaiticus, retaining its unaccented spelling and word division. It is not a photographic facsimile or a complete reproduction of the manuscript's original lineation.

**Revelation 1:14**

```
η δε κεφαλη αυτου και αι τριχες λευκαι ως εριο-
λευκον ως χιων και οι οφθαλμοι αυτου ως φλοξ πυρος
```

**Revelation 1:15**

```
και οι ποδες αυτου ομοιοι χαλκολιβανω ως εν καμινω
πεπυρωμενω και η φωνη αυτου ως φωνη ϋδατων πολλων
```

The hyphen after `εριο-` represents the division of the word across the transcription's line layout. It is not a lexical hyphen.

### Normalised transcription

**Revelation 1:14**

ἡ δὲ κεφαλὴ αὐτοῦ καὶ αἱ τρίχες λευκαὶ ὡς ἔριον λευκὸν ὡς χιών· καὶ οἱ ὀφθαλμοὶ αὐτοῦ ὡς φλὸξ πυρός.

**Revelation 1:15**

καὶ οἱ πόδες αὐτοῦ ὅμοιοι χαλκολιβάνῳ ὡς ἐν καμίνῳ πεπυρωμένῳ καὶ ἡ φωνὴ αὐτοῦ ὡς φωνὴ ὑδάτων πολλῶν.

_Note:_ The normalised text adds conventional accents, breathings and punctuation. It does not silently replace the manuscript's distinctive reading `πεπυρωμένῳ` with the more familiar alternative `πεπυρωμένοι`.

### Translation

**Revelation 1:14**

"But his head and his hairs were white, like white wool, like snow; and his eyes were like a flame of fire."

**Revelation 1:15**

"And his feet were like fine bronze, as though heated in a furnace; and his voice was like the sound of many waters."

The English word "were" is supplied to make the Greek construction idiomatic. The eye clause does not contain an explicit verb meaning "were".

## Editorial interventions

- The diplomatic transcription is based on the Codex Sinaiticus Project's electronic manuscript transcription.
    
- The electronic transcription was produced through independent initial transcriptions, followed by comparison and checking against manuscript images.
    
- No restoration of missing wording is required for the principal descriptions in these two verses as represented in the electronic transcription.
    
## Variants

### Revelation 1:14 – head and hair

Sinaiticus reads:

`η δε κεφαλη αυτου`

Normalised:

`ἡ δὲ κεφαλὴ αὐτοῦ`

This is the familiar reading, in contrast to the unusual `καὶ ἡ κεφαλή` reported for P98 by Hagedorn.

The manuscript then reads:

`και αι τριχες λευκαι ως εριον λευκον ως χιων`

The description of the head and hair as white, like wool and snow, is fully represented in the transcription.

### Revelation 1:14 – eyes

`και οι οφθαλμοι αυτου ως φλοξ πυρος`

The familiar wording is present. No distinctive variant affecting the description of the eyes is apparent in this reading.

### Revelation 1:15 – feet

Sinaiticus reads:

`και οι ποδες αυτου ομοιοι χαλκολιβανω`

The feet are described as like `χαλκολίβανον`, conventionally translated as fine or polished bronze.

The furnace comparison follows:

`ως εν καμινω πεπυρωμενω`

The important variant is:

- Sinaiticus (א): `πεπυρωμένῳ`
    
- Alexandrinus (A): `πεπυρωμένης`
    
- Textus Receptus: `πεπυρωμένοι`
    

These forms differ grammatically. The Sinaiticus form is dative singular, whereas the Textus Receptus form is nominative masculine plural.

The variation should be retained in the record rather than silently harmonising Sinaiticus with a later familiar text.

## Uncertainties

- The conventional mid-fourth-century date is widely accepted, but palaeographical dating cannot establish an exact year.
    
- A more cautious modern discussion allows a wider early-fourth- to early-fifth-century range.
    
- The manuscript's original geographical place of production is not securely established.
    
- The original extent and precise number of leaves are reconstructed from surviving material and codicological evidence.
    
- The transcription's normalisation should not be mistaken for the manuscript's original orthography, punctuation or layout.
    
- The meaning and precise material referent of `χαλκολίβανον` remain matters of lexical and interpretative discussion.
    
- The furnace comparison is a simile; it should not automatically be interpreted as a literal description of the material or colour of the feet.

- **Revelation 1:15:** Feet like fine bronze heated in a furnace; voice like many waters.
    
- **Revelation 2:18:** The description of the Son of God with eyes like a flame of fire and feet like fine bronze.
    
- **Ezekiel 1:24:** The sound of many waters, relevant to the imagery of Revelation 1:15.
    
- **Daniel 10:6:** Fiery eyes and shining bodily imagery, potentially relevant to the wider visual tradition.
