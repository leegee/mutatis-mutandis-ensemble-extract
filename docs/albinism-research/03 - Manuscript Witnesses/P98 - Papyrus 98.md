Wagner originally published the papyrus in 1971 as a non-biblical documentary text. Hagedorn identified the writing on the verso as part of Revelation in 1992 and supplied the first biblical transcription.

## Condition and physical characteristics

The surviving papyrus is damaged. The bracketed words in published transcriptions represent restorations or lost portions, not continuously legible letters.

The biblical writing is on the **verso**, the reverse of a previously used papyrus roll. The recto carries an earlier documentary text, apparently written by a different hand.

The reuse of the papyrus suggests economical production. Hagedorn considers the biblical text likely to be a private copy. The handwriting is irregular and not a conventional book hand, although it should not simply be classified as ordinary documentary cursive.

This makes P98 an important early witness, but not necessarily a representative example of a carefully produced ecclesiastical edition.

## Dating

The date is palaeographical. The date of the manuscript copy, the date of Revelation's composition, and the date of the underlying textual form are separate questions.

|Scholar/source|Proposed date|
|---|---|
|Wagner (1971)|Late first/second century for the recto handwriting|
|Hagedorn (1992)|Second century acceptable; beginning of third century possible|
|Orsini & Clarysse (2012)|c. 200–250 CE|
|Later scholarly summaries|Around 200 or first half of third century|

Orsini and Clarysse's reassessment moves P98 out of the second-century category and places it approximately in 200–250 CE.

[Orsini–Clarysse publication preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783161542688_A28632146/preview-9783161542688_A28632146.pdf)

**Working date:** c. 200–250 CE, following Orsini and Clarysse, while retaining Hagedorn's earlier dating as part of the scholarly history.

## Revelation 1:14 - Hair

### Hagedorn's diplomatic transcription

Hagedorn's published transcription records two separate surviving portions relevant to the passage:

|Manuscript line|Diplomatic transcription|
|---|---|
|Line 2|`]shn kai h k`e[`|
|Line 3|`] e`rion leukon `[`|

These are separate portions of the surviving papyrus, not a continuous transcription.

The brackets indicate lost or restored portions. The underdot indicates a letter read with uncertainty.

### Reconstructed textual context

For comparison, Hagedorn prints the continuous text from Nestle–Aland 26 (1984):

> ἡ δὲ κεφαλὴ αὐτοῦ καὶ αἱ τρίχες λευκαὶ ὡς ἔριον λευκὸν ὡς χιών

Translation:

> But his head and his hairs were white, like white wool, like snow.

**Important:** This is the continuous reference text, not a complete diplomatic transcription of P98.

### P98's distinctive reading

Hagedorn identifies the reading:

> καὶ ἡ κ̣ε\[φαλή

instead of the usual:

> ἡ δὲ κεφαλή

His comment on page 246:

> καὶ ἡ κ̣ε\[φαλή statt des üblichen ἡ δὲ κεφαλή scheint sonst nirgends überliefert zu sein.

Translation:

> καὶ ἡ κεφαλή instead of the usual ἡ δὲ κεφαλή seems to be otherwise unattested.

|Reading|Translation|Evidence|
|---|---|---|
|ἡ δὲ κεφαλή|But/as for the head|Hagedorn's comparison text, Nestle–Aland 26|
|καὶ ἡ κεφαλή|And the head|P98, according to Hagedorn|

This is a genuine textual variation, although Malik's 2016 revised transcription must be checked before treating Hagedorn's reading as definitive.

So the relevant words in the established text of Revelation 1:14 are:

|Greek|Transliteration|Meaning|
|---|---|---|
|λευκαί|leukai|White|
|ἔριον λευκόν|erion leukon|White wool|
|χιών|chiōn|Snow|

P98 physically preserves part of the wool comparison, including ἔριον λευκόν. The complete double comparison is established in the wider Greek textual tradition, but we must not claim that every component is physically preserved on this fragment.

**The significance of the double comparison**: the established literary text contains three components:

1. The hair is explicitly described as **white**.    
2. The first comparison is **white wool**.    
3. The second comparison is **snow**.    

The two comparisons are not a later English translation's embellishment; they belong to the established Greek text of Revelation.

For our manuscript record, however, the physical evidence of P98 must remain distinguished from the reconstructed context.

### Hagedorn's textual observations

Dieter Hagedorn, "P.IFAO II 31: Johannesapokalypse 1,13–20", _Zeitschrift für Papyrologie und Epigraphik_ 92 (1992), pp. 243–247.

[Full article and transcription](https://www.uni-koeln.de/phil-fak/ifa/zpe/downloads/1992/092pdf/092243.pdf)

|Revelation|P98 feature|Significance|
|---|---|---|
|1:13|Repeated μ in the participle|Scribal error|
|1:14|καὶ ἡ κεφαλή instead of ἡ δὲ κεφαλή|Unusual connective|
|1:16|Reading of the stars differs in grammatical form|Textual variation|
|1:17|Some words differ in spelling or form|Scribal/transmission evidence|
|1:18|Possible omission of καὶ ὁ ζῶν|Uncertain reconstruction|

At Revelation 1:18, Hagedorn considers whether καὶ ὁ ζῶν (_and the living one_) may have been omitted because the reconstructed line would otherwise be too long. He also considers whether the word πρῶτος (_first_) might have been represented by a numerical sign, which would affect the available space.

This remains an editorial hypothesis, not an established omission.

## Verification checklist

- Inspect the papyrus image.
    
- Verify the transcription character by character.
    
- Record uncertain letters, lacunae and restorations.
    
- Verify coverage and line references.
    
- Confirm the date range from the catalogue and relevant publications.
    
- Record edition, editor, publication details, and plate/page.
    
- Obtain and examine Malik's 2016 article.
    
- Compare P98 with other Greek witnesses and early versions.
    
- Establish which words of Revelation 1:14 are physically preserved, as opposed to restored from context.
