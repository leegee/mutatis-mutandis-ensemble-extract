# Evidence protocol

## Evidence layers

Every textual claim should be assigned one of these labels:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `MS` | Visible manuscript evidence |
| `TRANSCRIPTION` | Diplomatic or semi-diplomatic transcription |
| `EDITORIAL` | Editorial supplement, emendation, or reconstruction |
| `TRANSLATION` | Translation into a modern or ancient language |
| `COMMENTARY` | Ancient or modern interpretation |
| `INFERENCE` | Conclusion drawn from evidence |
| `HYPOTHESIS` | Proposed explanation not yet demonstrated |

## Transcription conventions

Adopt the conventions of the edition being cited. Do not silently mix:
- square brackets for lacunae;
- angle brackets for supplements;
- underdots for uncertain letters;
- editorial parentheses;
- normalised spelling;
- reconstructed words.

Explain the conventions at the start of each witness transcription.

## Dating protocol

For every date, record:
- date or range;
- what is being dated;
- method (palaeography, colophon, historical reference, radiocarbon, scholarly estimate);
- source;
- confidence.

Never transfer a manuscript date to the composition or translation date without independent evidence.

## Claim record

Recommended format:

**Claim:**
**Evidence layer:**
**Witness:**
**Exact location:**
**Transcription:**
**Translation:**
**Edition/catalogue:**
**Counter-evidence or uncertainty:**
**Status:**
**Researcher note:**

## Negative evidence

If a fragment does not preserve a word, state only that the word is not preserved in that fragment. Absence from a damaged witness is not proof that the original text lacked the word.
