## Colour terminology and interpretation

For every relevant colour expression, distinguish:

1. **Lexical meaning:** the historically attested semantic range of the original word.
    
2. **Grammatical construction:** morphology, syntax, comparison, coordination and the relationship between colour terms.
    
3. **Contextual appearance:** the physical appearance plausibly communicated by the expression in its particular passage.
    
4. **Translation:** the choices made by subsequent translators, including changes in colour terminology or intensity.
    
5. **Symbolic interpretation:** religious, literary, cultural or medical meanings attached to the appearance.
    

Do not assume that ancient colour categories map exactly onto modern English colour categories. In particular, distinguish lexical red/ruddy from a contextual interpretation of pink, rosy or flushed complexion.

Record whether a colour expression describes an individual characteristic or a relationship between multiple colours. Preserve the latter when tracing translation and reception.

A modern translation such as "pink" may communicate a plausible contextual appearance without being a literal lexical equivalent of the original term.
